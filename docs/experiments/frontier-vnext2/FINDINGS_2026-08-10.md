## What was actually run

- 49 trials: 43 completed, 2 CUDA OOM, 4 errors.
- Total measured trial time: 18,616.34 seconds (5.171 GPU-hours).
- Proxy corpus: 24,006,881 train tokens and 2,402,806 validation tokens, vocabulary 32,768.
- GPU: NVIDIA RTX 4080 Laptop, 12,282 MiB reported VRAM, Ada compute capability 8.9.
- Recorded runtime: PyTorch 2.13.0+cu132, Transformer Engine 2.17.0, FLA 0.5.1,
  Triton 3.7.1, TorchAO 0.17.0 and APOLLO 1.0.

This proxy is controlled architecture evidence, not the final pretraining corpus. The architecture
screens used only about 4.19M tokens at sequence length 2,048; they cannot support a final quality
verdict or a long-context claim.

## MoE utilization result

| Architecture screen | Median tok/s | Median GPU util | Median power | Median SM clock |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Dense, all MLA | 15,928.65 | 84% | 113.70 W | 2,340 MHz |
| Grouped MoE, KDA3 | 9,552.22 | 63% | 94.92 W | 2,400 MHz |
| Latent MoE, KDA3 | 8,064.74 | 59% | 84.05 W | 2,415 MHz |
| “Stable” latent MoE, KDA3 | 8,012.89 | 61% | 84.98 W | 2,415 MHz |

The low MoE utilization is real and abnormal for the intended result, but the evidence points to an
underfed GPU rather than thermal throttling: utilization and power fall while clocks remain similar.
At sequence length 2,048, micro-batch 1 and gradient accumulation 16, an 8-expert/top-2 layer has
roughly 512 routed token rows per expert per micro-batch; 16 experts/top-2 has roughly 256. Gradient
accumulation does not combine those rows into larger expert GEMMs. Kernel launch, routing, sorting,
padding, Python/host synchronization and many small GEMMs can therefore dominate.

The first code-level fixes following this audit are deliberately evidence-directed:

- removed repeated authoritative-weight binding in the Transformer Engine grouped expert path;
- replaced duplicate routing sort/index construction with one sort and derived token indices;
- replaced dense one-hot router-load accounting with `bincount`;
- removed unconditional device-to-host scalar extraction in latent-MoE load tracking;
- stopped collecting Python router summaries on non-logging steps;
- kept loss accumulation on device, eliminating up to five host synchronizations per micro-batch;
- combined GPU telemetry fields into one `nvidia-smi` sampling query and fixed overwritten trial names.

These are not yet performance claims. They must pass the matched benchmark protocol below.

## Attention conclusions (and non-conclusions)

- `dense-all-mla` is a control baseline, not the final architecture decision.
- The absorbed MLA A/B/B/A scout was about 4.7% faster than reconstructed MLA in that small systems
  test. It should remain the Ada baseline.
- KDA means Kimi Delta Attention. It is not DeepSeek Sparse Attention.
- Official FlashMLA/FlashKDA kernels target newer datacenter architectures in important paths; this
  laptop is Ada SM89. Hardware dispatch is required rather than pretending an SM90 kernel is usable.
- The “stable latent MoE” screen only changed `latent_moe_post_norm=true`. It was not a faithful Kimi
  K3 Stable LatentMoE implementation and therefore cannot reject that published architecture.

## Power interruption annotation

`dense-all-mla-lr3e-4` completed its entire 8,388,608-token budget and wrote its final checkpoint.
The brief unplug is visible in two roughly five-second telemetry samples (about 55 W at 1,185 MHz,
then about 54 W at 870 MHz) and two throughput dips at steps 224 and 228. Throughput immediately
returned from 11.6–12.2K tok/s to about 16K tok/s. The quality result and checkpoint remain valid;
median throughput is the robust performance statistic. Do not spend time rerunning it solely because
of the unplug—retain the annotation in comparisons.

## Failed/incomplete trials that must not be presented as quality evidence

- `dense-kda1-lr2e-4`: non-finite loss after 50% of intended tokens.
- `dense-kda3-lr4e-4`: non-finite loss after 54.6875% of intended tokens.
- DeepSeek-style MTP trials: failed at step 0 because Transformer Engine FP8 alignment rejected
  `[1, 2047, 1536]`. This is an integration failure, not evidence against MTP quality.
- 65,536 dense context and 8,192 grouped-MoE context systems probes: CUDA OOM.

## Performance gate for the next campaign

No architecture or kernel earns promotion from a single fastest number. Each candidate must use:

1. identical seed, data ordering, optimizer, schedule, tokens and validation set;
2. warm-up excluded from timing, at least 20 measured optimizer steps, and three repetitions where
   run cost permits;
3. tokens/s, update latency distribution, peak allocated/reserved VRAM, GPU utilization, power,
   tokens/joule, achieved TFLOP/s estimate, router balance/drop/overflow and compile time;
4. forward/backward/optimizer profiler traces for dense and MoE at matched active parameters;
5. validation loss/perplexity parity and deterministic checkpoint/resume verification;
6. separate training and inference Pareto fronts—prefill, decode, latency, throughput, memory and
   long-context retrieval quality are not interchangeable;
7. failure rows retained with completion fraction and failure class.

The immediate MoE matrix should vary micro-batch size, sequence length, experts/top-k, capacity,
grouped vs reference experts, and compile/precision backend one factor at a time. The primary question
is whether expert GEMM occupancy rises with routed rows per expert. Only after the trace identifies a
remaining hotspot should a custom Triton/CUDA kernel be considered. Existing PyTorch grouped GEMM,
Transformer Engine, FLA, FlashAttention/SDPA and compatible published kernels are benchmarked first.

## Research interpretation

The 100B-token goal is a training-budget target, not a guarantee of grokking. Grokking requires a
defined task/capability, held-out generalization curve and a reproducible transition; simply consuming
100B tokens is not sufficient. Checkpoints and evaluations must therefore be dense enough to study
capability growth, scaling behavior, data repetition, memorization and any delayed generalization
without retroactively inventing a claim.
